# 03 · Five Elements (오행, 五行)

The Five Elements are the **substance of change** in Saju. They are not physical elements; they are dynamic patterns of transformation observed in nature.

| Korean | Hanja | English | Season | Direction | Color (rough) | Taste | Senses | Organs | Yin (음) | Yang (양) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 목 (mok) | 木 | Wood | Spring | East | Green/Blue | Sour | Eyes, vision | Liver (간), Gallbladder (담) | 乙 (을) | 甲 (갑) |
| 화 (hwa) | 火 | Fire | Summer | South | Red | Bitter | Tongue, speech | Heart (심), Small intestine (소장) | 丁 (정) | 丙 (병) |
| 토 (to) | 土 | Earth | Late summer | Center | Yellow | Sweet | Mouth, taste | Spleen (비), Stomach (위) | 己 (기) | 戊 (무) |
| 금 (geum) | 金 | Metal | Autumn | West | White | Spicy/Pungent | Nose, smell | Lungs (폐), Large intestine (대장) | 辛 (신) | 庚 (경) |
| 수 (su) | 水 | Water | Winter | North | Black | Salty | Ears, hearing | Kidneys (신), Bladder (방광) | 癸 (계) | 壬 (임) |

> The body-organ correspondences are general guides for the **health-tendency** portion of a reading. They are **not** a medical diagnosis. See `CLAUDE.md` ground rules.

## The Generating Cycle (상생, 相生)

Each element generates the next:

```
Wood → Fire → Earth → Metal → Water → Wood
(목 → 화 → 토 → 금 → 수 → 목)
```

- **Wood generates Fire:** A fire needs wood as fuel.
- **Fire generates Earth:** Fire produces ash (earth).
- **Earth generates Metal:** Metal is mined from the earth.
- **Metal generates Water:** Metal condensation produces water (classical interpretation).
- **Water generates Wood:** Water nourishes the tree.

The element that "generates" is **drained** (its energy is spent), and the element that is "generated" receives support. In 십신 terms, the generator is the **인성 (Resource)** of the generated. See `05-ten-gods.md`.

## The Overcoming Cycle (상극, 相剋)

Each element overcomes (controls, restrains) another:

```
Wood → Earth → Water → Fire → Metal → Wood
(목 → 토 → 수 → 화 → 금 → 목)
```

- **Wood overcomes Earth:** Tree roots break up soil.
- **Earth overcomes Water:** Earth dams water.
- **Water overcomes Fire:** Water extinguishes fire.
- **Fire overcomes Metal:** Fire melts metal.
- **Metal overcomes Wood:** Axe cuts tree.

The element that "overcomes" is **exerted** (its energy is spent in the act), and the element that is "overcome" is **subdued**. In 십신 terms, the overrcomer is the **관성 (Officer)** of the overcome. See `05-ten-gods.md`.

## The Two Sub-Cycles

In addition to the main cycles, traditional 명리 recognizes two derivative sub-cycles:

### 1. Reverse Overcoming (역극, 反剋) — When the overcome resists

- Metal cannot cut wood that's too dense.
- Wood cannot penetrate earth that's too dry / hard.
- Water cannot extinguish fire that's too vast.
- Fire cannot melt metal that's too pure.
- Earth cannot dam water that's too strong.

A reverse 극 usually means **the controlling element is exhausted** or the controlled element is overwhelming. Often read as: the querent pushes back against a controlling force and gets stuck.

### 2. Same-Element Interaction (비화, 比和)

Same-element relationships — a stem of element X meeting another stem of element X — produce the **비겁 (Companion / Robbery)** class of 십신. Pure 비화 between two strong same-element stems produces a sense of **solidarity or competition** depending on the yin/yang match (see `05-ten-gods.md`).

## When Each Element Is Strong

| Element | Strong in Season | Strong in Branch | Strong by Combination |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wood | Spring (인/묘 month) | 寅, 卯, 亥, 未 (half) | 亥卯未 삼합, 인해 육합 |
| Fire | Summer (사/오 month) | 巳, 午, 寅, 戌 (half) | 寅午戌 삼합, 오미 육합 |
| Earth | Late summer (辰/未/戌/丑 months) | 辰, 戌, 丑, 未 | 辰戌丑未 (四庫) |
| Metal | Autumn (신/유 month) | 申, 酉, 巳, 丑 (half) | 巳酉丑 삼합, 진유 육합 |
| Water | Winter (해/자 month) | 亥, 子, 申, 辰 (half) | 申子辰 삼합, 사신 육합 |

## When Each Element Is Weak

- **Wood:** In Metal-dominant months (申, 酉) or when cut by strong Metal in the chart. Drought (no Water), winter (frozen).
- **Fire:** In Water-dominant months (亥, 子) or extinguished by strong Water. Winter, flooded conditions.
- **Earth:** In Wood-dominant months (寅, 卯) or eroded by strong Wood. Excess rain (Water over Earth), fire burning the soil.
- **Metal:** In Fire-dominant months (巳, 午) or melted by strong Fire. Summer.
- **Water:** In Earth-dominant months (辰, 戌, 丑, 未) or blocked by strong Earth. Drought, heat.

## 용신 (Favorable Element) — High-Level Logic

The **favorable element (용신)** is the element that **balances** the chart. The logic in classical 명리:

1. **Determine Day Master strength** — see `09-interpretation-method.md` step 2.
2. **If Day Master is strong (신강, 身強):** Favor the elements that **drain / control / exhaust** the Day Master: 식상 (output), 재성 (wealth), 관성 (authority).
3. **If Day Master is weak (신약, 身弱):** Favor the elements that **support** the Day Master: 인성 (resource), 비겁 (companions).
4. **Special grids (종격, 화격, etc.):** The Day Master's "own element" may be the enemy; in those cases, the rules shift — see `07-special-formations.md`.

## 희신 / 기신 / 구신 / 한신 (Supporting / Unfavorable / Restricting / Draining)

Around 용신, the other elements are classified:

- **용신 (Yongsin):** The favorable element — the most needed.
- **희신 (Huisin):** The element that **assists** 용신. Two conventions are in use (see the note below):
  - *Strict classical (적천수 lineage):* the element that **generates** 용신. E.g., if 용신 is Water, 희신 is Metal (Metal generates Water).
  - *Modern Korean 명리 (권인성·곽임성·정봉재·송기영 schools):* the **secondary favorable element** that shares 용신's balancing direction.
- **기신 (Gisin):** The element that **opposes** the chart's balance — the element the chart already has in excess, or that controls 용신. E.g., if 용신 is Water, 기신 is the element that harms Water's balancing role (commonly Fire, which Water overcomes, or Earth in excess which restrains Water).
- **한신 (Hansin):** The element that **drains** 용신. E.g., if 용신 is Water, 한신 is Wood (since Water generates Wood).
- **구신 (Gusin):** The element that **restrains** 용신. E.g., if 용신 is Water, 구신 is Earth (since Earth controls Water).

> **Two conventions for 희신 — when each applies.** The strict classical "희신 = the element that generates 용신" works cleanly when 용신 is a specific functional element that benefits from being nourished (typical in special grids 종격/화격 and in balanced charts where 용신 is the under-represented element). For ordinary **신강 (strong DM)** and **신약 (weak DM)** charts, the strict formula paradoxically yields the **기신** element:
>
> - **신강:** 용신 = 식상 (output). The element that generates output is **비겁 (self)** — but 비겁 is the 기신 for a strong DM (it echoes the over-strong self). So strict "generates 용신" gives the enemy.
> - **신약:** 용신 = 인성 (resource). The element that generates resource is **관성 (authority)** — but 관성 is the 기신 for a weak DM (it presses the weak self). Same paradox.
>
> For these reasons the **modern Korean convention** defines 희신 as the **secondary favorable element that works alongside 용신** in the same balancing direction: for a strong DM, 용신=식상 and 희신=재성 (both drain the strong self); for a weak DM, 용신=인성 and 희신=비겁 (both support the weak self). The engine in `src/saju_engine/strength.py` follows the **modern convention** for strong/weak charts and the **strict classical formula** for balanced charts (where it is unambiguous), and labels the result a heuristic candidate only. Some schools also swap 기신/구신; always confirm which convention a practitioner uses.
