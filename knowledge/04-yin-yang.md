# 04 · Yin-Yang (음양, 陰陽)

Yin and Yang are the **two primordial polarities** of all phenomena. They are not opposites in the sense of good-vs-evil; they are **complementary aspects** of one whole. In Saju, every stem, branch, element, and ten-god has a yin or yang polarity, and the balance of yin and yang is one of the two pillars of chart interpretation (the other being the Five Elements).

## Core Principles

1. **Taiji (태극, 太極) produces the two polarities.** From a single source of primordial qi, yin and yang emerge.
2. **Yin and yang are relative, not absolute.** A thing is yin or yang **in relation to** another thing. Yang in one context can be yin in another. E.g., Sun = yang, Moon = yin; but the Sun's surface (which absorbs) is yin to its rays (which emit).
3. **Yin and yang generate each other.** Yin reaches its peak and yields to yang; yang reaches its peak and yields to yin. This is the source of the waxing/waning cycle of seasons.
4. **Yin and yang are mutually consuming (음양소태, 陰陽消長).** When one grows, the other recedes. Excessive growth of one leads to injury of that one — yang at maximum must turn into yin, and vice versa.
5. **Yin-yang harmony (음양조화, 陰陽調和) is health.** Imbalance leads to disorder — too much of either is a problem.

## Yin / Yang by Stem and Branch

### Stems (천간)

| Yang (양, 陽) | Yin (음, 陰) |
|---|---|
| 甲 갑 (Yang Wood) | 乙 을 (Yin Wood) |
| 丙 병 (Yang Fire) | 丁 정 (Yin Fire) |
| 戊 무 (Yang Earth) | 己 기 (Yin Earth) |
| 庚 경 (Yang Metal) | 辛 신 (Yin Metal) |
| 壬 임 (Yang Water) | 癸 계 (Yin Water) |

The pattern: **odd-numbered stems are yang, even-numbered stems are yin.** 甲丙戊庚壬 (1, 3, 5, 7, 9) are yang; 乙丁己辛癸 (2, 4, 6, 8, 10) are yin.

### Branches (지지)

| Yang (양) | Yin (음) |
|---|---|
| 子 자 (Rat) | 丑 축 (Ox) |
| 寅 인 (Tiger) | 卯 묘 (Rabbit) |
| 辰 진 (Dragon) | 巳 사 (Snake) |
| 午 오 (Horse) | 未 미 (Goat) |
| 申 신 (Monkey) | 酉 유 (Rooster) |
| 戌 술 (Dog) | 亥 해 (Pig) |

The pattern: **yang branches are odd-indexed** (子, 寅, 辰, 午, 申, 戌); **yin branches are even-indexed** (丑, 卯, 巳, 未, 酉, 亥).

## Yang vs Yin in the Five Elements

Each element has a yang and a yin version:

| Element | Yang (양) | Yin (음) |
|---|---|---|
| Wood | 甲 갑 (great tree) | 乙 을 (vine) |
| Fire | 丙 병 (sun) | 丁 정 (candle) |
| Earth | 戊 무 (mountain) | 己 기 (field) |
| Metal | 庚 경 (axe) | 辛 신 (jewelry) |
| Water | 壬 임 (ocean) | 癸 계 (rain) |

The yang version is **active, bright, exposing, leading**; the yin version is **receptive, hidden, refined, supportive**.

## Yin-Yang in Personality

When the Day Master is **yang**:
- Tends to be direct, expressive, leading, outward-facing.
- Reads others as "spectators" of one's own projects.
- The self is more "public" — what one does is visible.

When the Day Master is **yin**:
- Tends to be indirect, intuitive, receiving, inward-facing.
- Reads others as "partners" in one's own projects.
- The self is more "private" — what one does is hidden.

A balanced chart has both yin and yang forces in roughly equal measure. The **year pillar** and **day pillar** are checked as a quick balance test:
- Year stem yang + day stem yang = "pure yang" tendency (very exposed, very active).
- Year stem yin + day stem yin = "pure yin" tendency (very hidden, very receptive).

## Yin-Yang in the Ten Gods

The yin-yang polarity decides the **specific** ten god within each class:

| Class | Yang version | Yin version |
|---|---|---|
| 비겁 (Companion) | 비견 (比肩, Pillar) | 겁재 (劫財, Robber) |
| 식상 (Output) | 식신 (食神, Eating God) | 상관 (傷官, Hurting Officer) |
| 재성 (Wealth) | 편재 (偏財, Indirect Wealth) | 정재 (正財, Direct Wealth) |
| 관성 (Authority) | 편관 (偏官, Seven Killings) | 정관 (正官, Direct Officer) |
| 인성 (Resource) | 편인 (偏印, Indirect Resource) | 정인 (正印, Direct Resource) |

> Example: 丙 (Yang Fire) appearing in the chart of an 甲 (Yang Wood) day master. 丙 generates 甲 (Fire feeds Wood), so 丙 is a **인성 (Resource)**. Because 丙 is **yang** and the Day Master 甲 is **yang**, same polarity → **편인 (Indirect Resource)**. If the Day Master were 乙 (Yin Wood), then 丙 would be a **정인 (Direct Resource)**.

## Yin-Yang in Time

The cycle of a day (and by extension the cycle of a year, a life):

- **子 (23:00–01:00):** Pure yin turning to yang. Mid-night. New yang begins to stir.
- **卯 (05:00–07:00):** Pure yang (sunrise). Mid-morning. Yang at first bloom.
- **午 (11:00–13:00):** Yang at peak. Mid-day. Sun at zenith.
- **酉 (17:00–19:00):** Pure yin (sunset). Mid-evening. Yin at first bloom.
- **亥 (21:00–23:00):** Yin at peak. Late night. Yang about to be reborn.

In a Saju reading, the **hour pillar** captures the "moment" of birth — what yin-yang energy was dominant at that time.

## Yin-Yang in Relationships

The pairing of yin and yang is the source of all relationship. In 명리:

- **Husband and wife** in traditional 명리 is sometimes encoded as the **day branch (배우자궁, 配偶者宮)** — the spouse's "palace" in the chart. The branch opposite it (the **clash branch**) is the "spouse's challenger."
- The **yin-yang match** between a day master and a partner's chart is read as: yang seeks yang for companionship; yin seeks yin for stability. (Or, in another reading: yang seeks yin for completion. Both views exist; classical texts favor the latter.)
- Same-element + same-polarity marriages (e.g. 甲 + 甲) are "pure yang" pairings — strong, but can be unstable. Mixed pairings (e.g. 甲 + 乙) are "yin-yang balanced" — gentler.

## Imbalance Indicators

| Pattern | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| All yang stems (year + month + day + hour all yang) | "Pure yang" — extremely exposed, active, possibly aggressive. May struggle with the receiving / listening side of life. |
| All yin stems | "Pure yin" — extremely hidden, receptive, possibly passive. May struggle with initiation. |
| Yang dominant (3 or 4 of 4) but not pure | Active, decisive, with occasional moments of withdrawal. |
| Yin dominant (3 or 4 of 4) but not pure | Reflective, perceptive, with occasional bursts of action. |
| Balanced (2 yang / 2 yin) | Classic harmony — strong and receptive in turn. |

For branches, the same logic applies. The 4-pillar yin-yang balance is **one of the most basic harmony tests** in classical 명리.
