## 2. Korean Saju apps (the home-market reference set)

These are the closest analogues — what Korean users expect, what the UX conventions are, what the price points look like.

### 2.1 사주닷컴 / 신점 (Saju Online)
- **Operator:** 사주닷컴 (stjpbl.com), founded 2001.
- **Reach:** 1M+ Google Play downloads; iOS App Store ~#67 Lifestyle.
- **Rating:** 4.4★ Play Store, 4.5★ App Store.
- **Features:** Saju 만세력 (calendar), 오늘의 운세 (daily horoscope), 토정비결 (Tojeong Bigyeol Korean fortune), 신점 (divination), 관상/타로/꿈해몽 (face reading / tarot / dream interpretation), 궁합 (compatibility), 신년 운세 (New Year fortune), 월별/일간 운세 (monthly/daily).
- **Pricing:** ad-supported free tier + paid premium subscription.
- **Position vs. us:** Broad, entertainment-leaning, Korean-language only, no English. Lots of features; quality of interpretation is mixed because each method is shallow.

### 2.2 당신의 사주 (Your Saju)
- **Reach:** Major Korean Saju app, comparable to 사주닷컴 in popularity.
- **Pricing:** Subscription + per-report pricing.
- **Position vs. us:** User-generated + curated readings. We have a stronger knowledge base and a more conservative (classical-tradition) interpretive stance.

### 2.3 만세력 달빛 (Dalbit / 365+ / Seezn)
- **Operator:** NICE Holdings (large Korean conglomerate).
- **Reach:** Multiple millions of downloads across the family of apps.
- **Features:** 만세력 (calendar) plus daily fortune, Tojeong, compatibility. Strong brand from a reputable parent.
- **Position vs. us:** Enterprise credibility but functionally similar to 사주닷컴.

### 2.4 유어사주 (Yoursaju)
- **Pricing:** **990원 (~$0.75) per reading** — the cheapest in the segment.
- **Model:** AI 사주 분석 (AI Saju analysis), 종량제 (pay-per-use).
- **Position vs. us:** Race-to-the-bottom on price; not a sustainable model for premium positioning.

### 2.5 운빨 사주 (Unbbal)
- **Pricing:**
  - **Pay-per-use 천기 (energy credits):** 1,000원 trial → 50,000원 premium pack
  - **Subscription tiers:**
    - Free: 0원/month
    - Basic: 9,900원/month (~$7.50)
    - Premium: 29,900원/month (~$22)
    - Pro: 49,900원/month (~$37)
    - Max: 99,000원/month (~$75)
- **Position vs. us:** The most developed pay-per-use + subscription hybrid in the Korean Saju space. Sets the ceiling for what Korean users will pay.

### 2.6 천명 (Chunmyung)
- **Pricing:** Per-consultation **촛불 (candle) credits** — 2,100원 to 71,500원.
- **Model:** Marketplace connecting users with verified tarot/신점/Saju readers. Platform commission 20–40%.
- **Reach:** 10만+ downloads, 25만+ reviews.
- **Position vs. us:** Human-reader marketplace, not algorithmic. Confirms the demand for human-delivered Saju consultation.

### 2.7 사주GPT
- **Reach:** MAU 250,000, 20%+ YoY growth (per 한국데이터신문).
- **Model:** AI Saju chatbot.
- **Position vs. us:** Pure-AI, low-quality interpretive layer (the "chatbot" UX pattern). The user gets generic responses, not classical-tradition-grounded interpretations.

### 2.8 Average Korean paid Saju consultation
- Per 한국데이터신문 reporting, average paid Saju consultation: **5,000–20,000원 (~$3.75–$15) for 5–10 minutes**.
- Top-tier readers can charge **30,000원+ per session**.
- This sets the reference price band: any Saju product under $5 is a low-end impulse buy; anything $15–75/month is a serious subscription; anything above needs to be a deep report.

## 3. English-language Saju apps (our actual competitive set)

This is the niche the project targets. The set is **shockingly thin**:

| App | Platform | Pricing | Quality | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Lucky Saju** | iOS | $3.99 one-time | Low–Medium | Most prominent paid English saju app. Too cheap to support ongoing development. |
| **The Saju Booth** | Web | Subscription (undisclosed) | Medium | Markets itself as "first premium saju reading platform in English." Limited public traction. |
| **Aha Chat** | iOS / Android | Freemium | Medium | AI chatbot, multilingual (English + Korean). Lacks the classical-tradition depth. |
| **Korean Saju Palja Chat** | iOS / Android | Freemium | Low | AI chatbot; very thin interpretation. |
| **마이 사주 (My Saju) international version** | iOS / Android | Freemium | Medium | Some English localization, but Korean-first. |

**The competitive vacuum here is real.** No well-funded, citation-grounded, English-first Korean Saju product exists at scale.

## 4. BaZi / Four Pillars apps (Chinese-language, the cultural analogue)

BaZi is the Chinese cognate of Korean Saju. The apps are similar in shape but culturally different in execution. The English-facing BaZi apps are the closest direct UX analogues for English-speaking users curious about East-Asian destiny traditions.

### 4.1 Purple Star Astrology (紫微斗數 — Zi Wei Dou Shu)
- **Reach:** ~50–100K iOS downloads [estimate].
- **Revenue:** ~$50–100K/year [estimate, single-developer].
- **Pricing:** Free tier + $4.99/month or $29.99/year subscription.

### 4.2 Lifesycle (formerly Purple Star Astrology by Yorkshine)
- **Reach:** 100–500K downloads iOS+Android [estimate].
- **Revenue:** ~$100–300K/year [estimate].

### 4.3 Master of Destiny / Destiny Master / Chinese Fortune Calendar
- Subscription range $6.99–$14.99/month, annual ~30–40% discount.

**Common BaZi app pattern:** freemium with a thin free tier (chart only), then 3-day to 7-day free trial, then $4.99–$14.99/month subscription gating interpretation layers. Annual plans at $19.99–$29.99/year.

## 5. Western astrology apps (the UX benchmark, not the content)

These are not direct competitors on **content**, but they set the UX bar and the price points English-speaking users are used to.

### 5.1 Co-Star (Co–Star Astrology Society)
- **Reach:** ~30.8M iOS downloads; ~2.7M MAU; ~64K paying users; 4.79★ (205K reviews).
- **Revenue:** ~$797.8K/month (~$9.57M/year) [estimate, Rev.now 2025].
- **Funding:** $20.95M total raised. Series A $15M led by Spark Capital (April 2021).
- **Pricing:**
  - Pro-Star (Monthly): $8.99/month
  - Advanced Chart – Relationships: $8.99/month
  - Advanced Chart – Self: $8.99/month
  - Add Manually: $2.99/week
  - Crush Report: $4.99/week
  - Eros: $6.99/week
  - Your Year Ahead: $11.99/year
  - 5 Questions: $2.99 one-time
- **Revenue mix:** ~72% subscription, ~22% IAP, ~6% ads.
- **Notable:** ~25% of US women aged 18–25 had downloaded Co-Star as of 2021.

### 5.2 The Pattern, Sanctuary, Chani, Time Passages, Astrology Zone
- All freemium subscription $5–$15/month, $40–$80/year.
- Sanctuary adds live human astrologer chat at ~$1.99/min on top.
- Time Passages is the most respected for serious chart calculation.
- Chani positions on queer/identity inclusivity, premium content drops.

**Benchmark takeaways:**
- $5–$15/month subscription is the established sweet spot for Western astrology apps.
- **The pattern of "free chart → paid interpretation" works** — the chart is the funnel, the reading is the product.
- "Your Year Ahead" at $11.99/year is the most-cloned feature across this category.
- The Western astrology audience expects AI-generated daily horoscopes and push notifications; they don't expect hand-delivered long-form readings.

## 6. Indian astrology apps (Vedic Jyotish — the contested market)

This is the biggest single-country market by revenue. It is **head-to-head hostile territory** for a Korean Saju entrant — but worth understanding because the demand patterns are instructive.

### 6.1 AstroTalk (the market leader)
- **Reach:** 30M+ users; 4.5M paid sessions/month + 6M free sessions/month.
- **Revenue:** **₹651 crore FY24** (doubled YoY), net profit ₹94 crore. IPO planned 2026, targeting ₹2,000 crore.
- **Pricing:** ₹10–200/min for chat consultations; ~20% of revenue from international markets.
- **Network:** 13,000+ astrologers on platform.
- **Business model:** Per-minute chat is dominant; subscription is emerging.

### 6.2 InstaAstro
- **Reach:** 5M+ users; 200K+ monthly installs; 50,000 minutes/day.
- **Revenue:** ARR ~$5M; ₹18.5 crore pre-Series A (May 2024, Artha Venture Fund lead).
- **Pricing:** Per-minute chat + per-question tarot.
- **Demographics:** 61% Gen Z; 58.67% of Gen Z queries are relationship-related; ~70% prefer chat.

### 6.3 GaneshaSpeaks, Astroyogi, AstroSage, Clickastro, AskGanesha, Astrosure.ai
- Long-standing players (GaneshaSpeaks 2003, Astroyogi 2004).
- Mix of subscription, per-question, and free-with-ads models.
- Astroyogi reports 60%+ Gen Z user base.

### 6.4 What Korean Saju can learn from AstroTalk
1. **Gen Z is the dominant growth segment.** AstroTalk doubled FY24 revenue by doubling down on Gen Z.
2. **Per-minute chat is the dominant monetization in India.** Subscription has not overtaken chat in India.
3. **AI-generated daily horoscope is the funnel.** Most Indian users start with free daily content and convert to chat.
4. **Compatibility / marriage matchmaking is huge.** "Kundli Milan" is one of the highest-volume search terms in Indian SEO.
5. **Vedic credibility is the moat.** AstroTalk wins because it has a deep bench of human Jyotish readers. A Korean Saju product in India must have a defensible classical-tradition positioning to not be seen as "foreign gimmick."

### 6.5 What Korean Saju should NOT copy
1. **Per-minute chat.** This requires a reader network. A solo founder or small team cannot build this.
2. **Heavy Vedic terminology overlap.** Don't pretend to be Vedic; differentiate on Korean classical tradition.
3. **Race-to-the-bottom pricing.** Indian market has trained users to expect ₹10–20/min. Premium Korean Saju should not undercut.

## 7. Where the Saju project slots in

| Dimension | Saju project positioning |
|---|---|
| **Content tradition** | Korean 명리 (Gyeonglakgoyo / Jeokcheon-su / Yeonhae-japyeong / Gungtong-bogam). Citation-grounded, classical-tradition-leaning. |
| **Language** | English-first with Korean/Hanja translated inline. Designed for international + diaspora + Hallyu-curious audience. |
| **Pricing** | One-time report (Spark ₹499, Reading ₹1,499, Full Map ₹3,499) + future Companion (₹799/month). Not per-minute chat. |
| **Interpretation** | Engine + human-reviewer for Reading/Full Map. Spark is fully automated. Companion is the retainer model. |
| **UX shape** | One-shot report purchase. No daily-horoscope feed (yet). No social features. |
| **Geography** | Primary: India (Indian Hallyu-curious + Tier-1 cosmopolitans). Secondary: Korean diaspora (US/CA/AU/UK). Tertiary: global Hallyu Wave audience. |

## 8. Competitive advantages (defensible)

1. **The calculation engine + classical-tradition knowledge base** — this took 18 months of work and is **cited from primary 명리 texts**, not generated by an LLM. Most competitors' interpretation is either LLM-prompt-driven (low quality) or copied from generic Chinese BaZi templates (wrong tradition).
2. **The premium tiered product with three distinct price points** — most competitors have one price point or pay-per-credit. The 4–5 / 10–12 / 18–22 page bundle structure is differentiated.
3. **English-first with proper Hanja/Korean translation** — almost no competitor does this well.
4. **Citation audit trail** — every interpretive claim can be traced to a `knowledge/*.md` file. This is the credibility moat for the fullmap tier (₹3,499) where the client expects depth.
5. **Self-service calculator is already built** — the PDF pipeline from intake → chart → tiered report is working end-to-end. Most competitors are still hand-delivering.

## 9. Competitive vulnerabilities (must address)

1. **No daily horoscope feed / retention loop.** Co-Star and AstroTalk retain users daily; we lose them after the one-shot report. → Companion subscription fixes this.
2. **No mobile app.** Most competitors have Android apps with millions of installs. → PWA → Capacitor fixes this.
3. **No brand / SEO presence.** 사주닷컴 has 24 years of brand equity. We have zero. → Content marketing fixes this, but slowly.
4. **No live chat / human network.** AstroTalk has 13,000 astrologers. We have one (the founder). → Out of scope; design the product around hand-delivery, not marketplace.
5. **Korean Saju is a niche.** Even in India, Vedic Jyotish is 100× the volume. → Position as premium / different / classical, not as Vedic competitor.
6. **English-only is a real constraint** for Indian market where Hindi + English + regional language dominate. → Hindi is the next-language addition after English stabilizes.

## 10. Strategic positioning summary

**The Saju project should position as "the citation-grounded English-first Korean 명리 reading service."** It is not trying to be Co-Star (Western astrology, daily app, Gen Z retention) and it is not trying to be AstroTalk (Vedic marketplace, per-minute chat, mass market). It is the **premium niche product for people who specifically want classical Korean Saju in English, with a defensible interpretive layer** — and it should charge accordingly.

**What to NOT do:**

- Don't enter the Indian mass market head-to-head against AstroTalk. The unit economics don't work for a solo founder.
- Don't try to compete with Korean Saju apps in Korea. The market is saturated and we cannot win on language.
- Don't pretend to be Vedic Jyotish. The traditions are different and Indian Jyotish practitioners will spot the difference immediately.
- Don't dilute into Western astrology. The classical-tradition positioning is the moat.
