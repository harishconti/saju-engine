8. What Is Missing

Stated plainly — these are the group's structural gaps, not cosmetic observations.

  • Metal function absent (Quality / finance / structure / compliance — the refining discipline): hire or partner this in; do not improvise it.

  • Water function absent (Audience / communication / regulation / flow — the information and market layer): hire or partner this in; do not improvise it.

  • Shared-money risk (peer-drains-wealth) is the top internal hazard. 2 of 2 members (Park, Seo) carry three or more Companion stems. The classical reading is that the Companion class competes for the same Wealth (재성) — financial loss and partnership breakdown, especially where money is shared: family businesses, pooled investments, informal lending among peers.

  • Capital depth. No member is Wealth-dominant — the funding call is a business problem, not a chart strength. The Companion-heavy charts also raise the cost of shared capital.

  • A single decisive leader. All members read balanced rather than strong; the Fire/public-face seat is the closest to a natural leader, but decision-rights must be written, not assumed.

  • Conflict management. The red flags in §2 (day-branch clash (충)/Hae (Pig) (해) and one broken day-stem combination) are the specific fault lines; name them in the operating agreement before money moves.

Structural Mitigations

  • Put roles and ownership percentages in writing before any money moves; the sibling and spouse pairings make informal assumptions likely.
  • Keep separate accounts; never commingle personal, family and venture funds.
  • Earn through a structured product or service with clear pricing, not informal arrangements.
  • Prefer hard-boundary roles over loosely-shared P&L; the group can collaborate without a shared wallet.
  • Treat any Companion (비겁)/Unfavorable Element (기신) decade (see each member's luck-cycle report) as a period to defend rather than expand shared commitments.
